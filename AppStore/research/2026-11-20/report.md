# Earn Ratings First, Then Free Every Drink

WaterBuddy's problem is visibility, not product. On 2026-11-20, 77 days after its first release and a week after version 1.4, it had **0 ratings in all eight storefronts measured**, sat **outside the top 50 on all 42 English searches** in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, and matched no Russian search at all ([Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6807675132&country=us)). By the owner's playbook, two self-inflicted causes dominate: a rating prompt that waits for a third straight goal day reached by a pour on Home, a moment the widget, Watch and Control Center loggers the app is built for rarely reach, and a name that leads with "WaterBuddy", a brand six other apps share, while apps with 0 ratings whose names *start* with "Water Intake" or "Hydration" make the US top 10. The 20 incumbents (1,373 to 165,063 US ratings, led by Waterllama, Drink Water Reminder N Tracker and Plant Nanny) all give water logging away and charge for drink libraries, custom cups, characters and ad removal, so WaterBuddy's free tier, with every quick-logging surface, no ads and no account, is the most generous in the set. In 3,500 US reviews, paywalls lead the complaints (31% of recent one- and two-star reviews), drinks other than water are the most resented lock (51 of 84 complaints about paying for basic logging), and people pay most happily once, about $10 for a discounted lifetime, for cuteness, drink variety and customisation; history, insights, export and recaps, the heart of WaterBuddy's Premium, almost never come up. The next version should therefore rewrite the listing: the name "Water Tracker - WaterBuddy", the subtitle "Stay Hydrated: Reminder Widget", Russian and English (U.K.) listings, widgets and the Watch in the first three screenshots, prices tiered by income, and a fix for a paywall that shows a 20% yearly saving instead of 44% wherever numbers use a decimal comma. The next build should make tea, coffee, juice and milk free to log, then sell caffeine tracking, alternate icons and themes. Widening the rating prompt, themes, more than three vessels, a goal raised by workouts, a Live Activity, iCloud sync and writing caffeine to Apple Health each amend a project rule and need the owner's approval first, and Live Activity and iCloud also lack measured demand. Expectations should stay modest: no hydration app makes the US Health & Fitness top-grossing 100, the leader is estimated at $100K–$160K a month, and the median new subscription app earns about $72 a month a year after launch.

## Zero ratings and a brand-first name keep WaterBuddy out of search

The playbook orders the work as discoverability first, then conversion, then payments, and starts from Apple's Lookup, where a rating count of zero is the headline, because in its words "without ratings Apple ranks an app under every competitor" (the playbook: the owner's `aso` skill, `SKILL.md`). WaterBuddy's Lookup shows version 1.4 (released 2026-11-13; first release 2026-09-04), Health & Fitness, free, app languages English, Russian and Uzbek. It also shows **0 ratings, average 0, in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, Russia, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan**, with a single English (U.S.) listing serving all of them ([us](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6807675132&country=us), [ru](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6807675132&country=ru), [uz](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6807675132&country=uz)). Area by area, the playbook's verdict is:

### The head terms are out of reach, but softer phrases are open

The playbook's rank script was run one term at a time. WaterBuddy sits outside the top 50 on every answered English search in every English storefront. On the US head terms (water tracker, water reminder, drink water, hydration, hydration tracker, water intake, drink reminder, hydration reminder) it is not even among the 181–193 apps Apple's Search API returns at its limit of 200 ([water tracker, US, limit 200](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=water+tracker&entity=software&country=us&limit=200)). Its best rank outside its own brand is **#86 of 187 for "hydration watch"** ([hydration watch](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=hydration+watch&entity=software&country=us&limit=200)). Difficulty here is the median rating count of a term's top 10 apps; for the US head terms it runs from 23,000 to 52,000. Taking the median across the seven head terms gives **25,135 ratings in the US, 2,734 in the UK, 2,541 in Canada and 1,183 in Australia**: the same words are about a tenth as hard in the UK and Canada and a twentieth as hard in Australia ([water tracker, GB](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=water+tracker&entity=software&country=gb&limit=50), [water tracker, AU](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=water+tracker&entity=software&country=au&limit=50)).

Two measurements make the gap closable. First, relevant long-tail phrases are led by apps whose names hold them, with almost no ratings. "Stay hydrated" has a US top-10 median of 20 and is led by a 0-rating app called Stay Hydrated. "Water diary" (2,340 in the US, 35 in the UK), "water reminder widget" (5,244 in the US, 378 in Australia, named by none of the top 10), "hydration widget" and "fluid intake" are similarly soft ([stay hydrated, US](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=stay+hydrated&entity=software&country=us&limit=50), [water diary, GB](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=water+diary&entity=software&country=gb&limit=50)). Second, **names that start with a searched phrase are ranked even with zero ratings**. "Hydration – Tracker" is #8 for "hydration tracker", "Water Intake Tracker" #9 for "water intake" and "Drink Water MicroApp" #8 for "drink water" ([hydration tracker](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=hydration+tracker&entity=software&country=us&limit=200), [water intake](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=water+intake&entity=software&country=us&limit=200)). WaterBuddy, whose name contains "Hydration Tracker" after the brand, misses the top 193 for that same search.

Two limits apply. How often each term is searched is unmeasured: Apple's suggestion feed no longer answers, and neither Astro nor an Apple Search Ads account was available. So "valuable" rests on proxies: how many top-10 apps put a term in their names, the leaders' ratings, and what searchers mean by the term. The Search API is also only a proxy for the App Store app's own ranking.

### The name and subtitle break the playbook's two weightiest field rules

All three fields fit Apple's limits: name 30/30 characters, subtitle 30/30, keyword field 98/100 bytes. They pass every mechanical check (no spaces, repeats, plurals or banned words) ([keywords.txt](../../../AppStore/keywords.txt)). They fail on substance.

The playbook puts the main keyword first and the brand second, and lets only an app that people search by name lead with its brand. "WaterBuddy" is not such a brand. Six other apps carry "Water Buddy" or "WaterBuddy" in their names, one of them released on 2026-11-18. The one-word search "waterbuddy" ranks WaterBuddy **fourth, behind three namesakes with 0–1 ratings** ([waterbuddy](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=waterbuddy&entity=software&country=us&limit=200)).

"Water", the category's main word, exists in the searchable pool only inside the compound. Probes suggest Apple does split it: "hydration buddy" finds the app at #92, and "buddy" appears nowhere else in its metadata ([hydration buddy](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=hydration+buddy&entity=software&country=us&limit=200)). But no pairing of "water" with another of its words retrieved it in the top 200, while the same pairings with "hydration" did. So the split, if real, earns nothing measurable. The phrase the name does bet on, "hydration tracker", appears in the names of only 1 of the top 10 apps in the US, UK and Canada (2 in Australia). "Water tracker" and "water reminder" appear in 5 to 8 of 10 across the four stores, which shows what other developers believe people type.

The subtitle "One tap. No account. No cloud." is a slogan, which the playbook rules out, and none of its words begins a water search. The keyword field spends bytes on "health" (broad), "habit" (a 149,063-rating habit tracker sits in the US top 10 for "water habit"), "private" (no measured search) and "glass". The best case for keeping the slogan is that subtitles appear in search results and the privacy promise is true. But a subtitle can only convert people who see the listing in results. Privacy is also now common indie positioning ([WaterTrack](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6752625006), [Hydrate](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760869789)), and reviewers almost never praise it. It belongs on screenshot 1's existing "No account · No cloud" chip, in the promotional text and in the description, which people read.

The research team counted the replacement sets by script against Apple's limits (the owner's `aso` skill, `references/apple-rules.md`):

| Field | Live 1.4 (en-US) | Recommended en-US (set 1) | en-GB variant | Russian (R1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | WaterBuddy - Hydration Tracker (30) | **Water Tracker - WaterBuddy** (26) | as set 1 | **Трекер воды: WaterBuddy** (23) |
| Subtitle | One tap. No account. No cloud. (30) | **Stay Hydrated: Reminder Widget** (30) | as set 1 | **Напоминание пить воду, баланс** (29) |
| Keyword field | drink,intake,reminder,goal,daily,glass,bottle,cup,log,widget,watch,streak,private,h2o,health,habit (98 bytes) | drink,hydration,intake,watch,daily,diary,log,fluid,habit,h2o,goal,streak,bottle,counter,balance,cup (99) | drink,hydration,intake,watch,daily,diary,log,fluid,habit,hydro,goal,streak,bottle,drinking,balance (98) | водный,норма,питья,дневник,виджет,часы,учет,пей,h2o,вода (100 bytes) |

Set 1 follows the playbook's choosing step literally. "Water Tracker" opens the name, which matches what the app is and puts the phrase where the measurements show ranking weight; "Water" stands as a word of its own. The subtitle carries "stay hydrated" exactly, plus "reminder widget", which with the name forms "water reminder widget", "water reminder" and "water widget", all real features. The keyword field keeps "drink" and "hydration" so that "drink water", "drink reminder" and "hydration tracker" still form across fields, and it adds the soft long tail (diary, fluid, intake, daily, balance, log).

A second set with the subtitle "Drink Reminder & Hydration Log" gives up the soft phrases for head terms. It becomes the better choice once ratings let WaterBuddy compete on those terms. The en-GB variant swaps in "hydro" and "drinking". In the UK and Australia "hydro" brings up Hydro Coach, a water app; in the US it brings up a rowing machine and in Canada a power utility. "Drinking water reminder" is soft in the UK (548) ([hydro, GB](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=hydro&entity=software&country=gb&limit=50), [hydro, CA](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=hydro&entity=software&country=ca&limit=50)).

Three cautions apply. Nobody checked whether "Water Tracker - WaterBuddy" is free as an App Store name. Apple documents neither word stemming nor whether an en-GB listing's keywords are read in Australia. And these fields change only with a new version, so they ride on the next submission. The research should be written down beside the fields, as the playbook asks, and the rank script rerun monthly for "water tracker", "stay hydrated", "water reminder widget", "water diary" and "fluid intake".

### The rating prompt waits for a moment most users never reach

Rule 70-privacy fixes the review request to one moment. It comes after the user's own pour on Home reaches the goal on the third day in a row, at most once per version, after the confetti, and never from the widget, Siri, the control, a reminder's button, the Watch or History ([rule 70-privacy](../../../.claude/rules/70-privacy.md)). That obeys every line of the playbook's ratings section: a happy moment, once per version, a tested function, off in tests. But the filters multiply. A user must stay three days, meet the goal three days running, and cross the third goal with a pour on Home rather than from the widget, Lock Screen, Control Center or Watch the listing promotes. Only then do Apple's display cap (three times a year, by the playbook's account) and the user's choice apply. With a small install base, that arithmetic predicts zero ratings after 77 days.

The leaders show the opposite failure. Their recent review streams are 85% four- and five-star and mostly short, and part of that is manufactured. In 17 recent US reviews people mention review prompts, gating or rewards: six Drink Water Reminder N Tracker reviewers say a review is demanded before features or history unlock, and five Plant Nanny reviewers mention a pot given for reviewing ([Phoenix reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=870372885/sortby=mostrecent/json), [Plant Nanny reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1424178757/sortby=mostrecent/json)). Rewarded reviews are what the playbook warns get apps removed.

The fix sits between the two. Ask at the **first** goal reached by a pour on Home, which is the playbook's "a goal reached" and the "first habit done" of the developer it learned from, or at the latest on the second goal day. Also allow the moment when the app is opened, after the vessel fills, on a day the goal was already reached from the widget or the Watch. Keep the once-per-version cap, the tested function and the DEBUG opt-in. **This amends rule 70-privacy and needs the owner's approval before code.** Three steps need no approval: reply to every review, add a gentle line to support replies, and point the support page at the rating link. Ratings count per storefront. The UK, Canada and Australia, at a tenth of US difficulty or less, are where the first few dozen will show soonest.

## The page, the prices and the languages leak the visitors search sends

### Screenshots never show logging without opening the app

The set is complete and honest. There are ten real iPhone captures in both required sizes, live in the intended order, plus one plain Watch capture with no headline, as the playbook asks ([Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6807675132&country=us), [screenshots](../../../AppStore/screenshots/iPhone-6.9/)). The first three are the one-tap vessel, the week card and a Premium-badged month, and together they say "a water log with charts". **None of the ten shows the app's differentiator**: logging from a Home Screen or Lock Screen widget, from Control Center, from a reminder's *Log a Glass* button or from a Watch complication. Yet that is the promise the promotional text and description open on ([description.txt](../../../AppStore/description.txt)). Six of the ten carry a Premium badge, the third among them, so a visitor meets the paywall before the free core. The tilted frames, lifted panes and handwriting over one aurora are also busier than the playbook's phone frame on a solid colour; the playbook's source watched a polished set lose an A/B test to a plainer one.

Those missing surfaces are exactly where the competition is thin. Among the top 20, only three apps log from a widget, three from Control Center or the Action Button, and none mentions logging from a notification ([Water Log](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1114617196), [Waterllama](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1454778585)). The Watch is the one platform feature reviewers praise often, 45 times at WaterMinder alone ([WaterMinder reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=653031147/sortby=mostrecent/json)). WaterBuddy's own privacy label was not checked, but if it reads "Data Not Collected", none of the top 20 matches it. Among the most-rated, only Waterllama avoids tracking data, and twelve of the twenty declare data "Used to Track You".

The new order: keep shot 1. Make shot 2 a Home Screen with the small and week widgets beside the Lock Screen widget ("Log without opening the app"). Make shot 3 the Watch with a complication, or a reminder with its button. Move Premium to shots 4–8 and add a second Watch shot on the Modular or Infograph face to show the three complications 1.4 shipped. Test the new order against today's with Product Page Optimization once the page has visitors.

### Equalised prices broke the yearly saving, and the paywall misreports it

The owner set US prices ($2.99 a month, $19.99 a year with a one-week trial, $39.99 lifetime) and let App Store Connect fill in every other country ([in-app-purchases.md](../../../AppStore/in-app-purchases.md)). At the ECB and Bank of Russia rates of 19–20 November, every storefront sits at **0.99× to 1.35× the US price**. Kazakhstan pays 1.35×, Uzbekistan is billed in US dollars at exactly the US price, and nothing is tiered by income ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml), [CBR](https://www.cbr.ru/scripts/XML_daily.asp), [KZ page](https://apps.apple.com/kz/app/id6807675132)). Connect's rounding also broke the ratio between plans: the yearly plan saves **44.3% in the US but 35.9% in Germany, 40.1% in Russia and 51.8% in Mexico** ([DE page](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/id6807675132), [MX page](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/id6807675132)).

Worse, wherever numbers use a decimal comma, the paywall's yearly tile shows a 20% saving instead of 44%. That covers the Russian, Uzbek, Kazakh, German, Brazilian, Turkish and Indonesian formats, and the owner's own note traces it to prices read as whole numbers (#162) ([in-app-purchases.md](../../../AppStore/in-app-purchases.md)). The bug understates the offer in exactly the markets the app's languages target, touches no rule, and should be fixed first. After that come the playbook's three tiers, set by the World Bank's income groups and worked out to keep each plan's ratio:

| Tier (storefronts in the sample) | Monthly | Yearly | Lifetime | Yearly saves | Lifetime ÷ yearly |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High income, full price (US, UK, Germany, Russia) | $2.99 | $19.99 | $39.99 | 44.3% | 2.00 |
| Upper-middle, about 40% off (Türkiye, Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico, Kazakhstan) | $1.79 | $11.99 | $23.99 | 44.2% | 2.00 |
| Lower-middle and low, about 70% off (India, Uzbekistan) | $0.89 | $5.99 | $11.99 | 43.9% | 2.00 |

Sources: the owner's `aso` skill, `references/price-tiers.md`; [World Bank groups](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/906519-world-bank-country-and-lending-groups).

The $0.89 breaks the playbook's .49/.59 endings below a dollar because $0.99 would make the yearly plan "save 50%". Nobody checked that these price points exist in App Store Connect for both subscriptions and the lifetime purchase. Once a tier is set, read every storefront's proposed prices and hand-fix any whose yearly saving drifts outside 43–45%; in the euro stores that means €3.49 a month or €19.99 a year. A price cut can't be taken back, and existing subscribers renew at it, so now, with almost no subscribers, is the cheapest moment to set tiers. Apple also recommends "Water Reminder: Ad Free", a rival with no purchases at all, on WaterBuddy's own product page ([US page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6807675132)).

The US list prices should stay. $2.99 and $19.99 sit at or below the category norm and well below the Health & Fitness medians of $9.99 a month and $39.94 a year, and data across all categories finds that higher-priced plans convert and earn better ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). The one price worth testing once traffic exists is the lifetime. It sits above Water Log's $29.99 and Waterllama's probable $19.99, and at about four times the roughly $10 discounted lifetime Waterllama buyers praise in reviews ([Water Log page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1114617196), [Waterllama reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1454778585/sortby=mostrecent/json)).

### A Russian and an English (U.K.) listing reach markets where hydration apps earn

With en-US the only localization, Russia, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan all see English, even when Russian is requested. On the Russian and German web pages, the line under the name shows the category instead of the subtitle ([ru, lang=ru_ru](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6807675132&country=ru&lang=ru_ru), [ru page](https://apps.apple.com/ru/app/id6807675132)). Apple's table settles what each listing can reach: **Russia shows Russian; Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan show only English (U.K.); and Apple has no Uzbek or Kazakh listing language** ([Apple, App Store localizations](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-store-localizations)).

A Russian listing would pay off in Russia, where WaterBuddy matches no search today. The head terms there are held at top-10 medians of about 5,000–7,000 ratings, between the UK and the US. Soft phrases can carry it while ratings come: "трекер питья" has a median of 248, and "баланс воды" is led by a 40-rating app of that name. Russian word forms are matched separately, so R1 carries воды, воду and вода ([трекер воды, RU](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%BA%D0%B5%D1%80+%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%B4%D1%8B&entity=software&country=ru&limit=50)). The money signal backs this. On 2026-11-20 Waterllama was the only hydration app in the Health & Fitness top-grossing 100 of **Russia (#81), Uzbekistan (#58) and Kazakhstan (#69)**, and it also charted in Türkiye (#95), Taiwan, China and Korea, where one Chinese water app joined it in Taiwan and China. No hydration app charted in the other storefronts checked, the US, UK, Germany, Japan and Brazil among them ([RU grossing](https://itunes.apple.com/ru/rss/topgrossingapplications/limit=200/genre=6013/json), [UZ grossing](https://itunes.apple.com/uz/rss/topgrossingapplications/limit=200/genre=6013/json), [US grossing](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/topgrossingapplications/limit=200/genre=6013/json)). Paid-upfront water apps at $1–$4 equivalents also chart in the Russian, Kazakh and Uzbek paid charts ([KZ paid](https://itunes.apple.com/kz/rss/toppaidapplications/limit=200/genre=6013/json)). Whether Russian users can pay through the App Store today was not researched.

An en-GB listing using set 1 is the cheaper second step. It needs no translation, and it is what the UK, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan and Türkiye show, as well as Australia while there is no en-AU listing. Russian searches in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan are barely contested, with top-10 medians of 0–1,034 and 77–207. Reaching them would mean either spending two bytes per Cyrillic letter in an en-GB keyword field that the UK also reads, or writing the en-GB listing in Russian, as the Kazakh leader evidently does, which would cost the UK. That trade is the owner's to make ([KZ leaders](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1440323599,1208916325,1476595833&country=kz)). Uzbek keywords are a low-value bet: **10 of 12 Uzbek searches return no app at all**, which can't tell "nobody searches" from "nobody targets" ([suv ichish, UZ](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=suv+ichish&entity=software&country=uz&limit=50)).

The category is mature and concentrated. The top five hold about 540,000 US ratings, and every app averages 4.40–4.91, so star averages set nobody apart. Four of the top twelve (Healive, both Grassapper apps and Gismart) have not shipped since 2016–2022 and live on old counts. Waterllama is the reference rival on both search and product: it is in the top 8 for all nine generic terms measured and #1 for "water tracker", "hydration" and "hydration tracker" ([Search API, "hydration"](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=hydration&entity=software&country=us&limit=50)). Most incumbents also run on iPad (15 of 20), Apple-silicon Macs (17) and the Watch (14). Their median is about 16 languages; WaterBuddy has 3.

A few features are table stakes and usually free: charts and history (20 of 20), reminders (20), a Watch app, Apple Health, drinks beyond water, a Home Screen widget and a weight-based goal (14 each), and custom cups (13). Others are rare: Lock Screen widgets (6), Siri (4), logging from a widget (3), Control Center or the Action Button (3), a Live Activity (2: Waterllama's "dehydration mode" and Water Log's Pro), CSV export (1) and logging from a notification (0). WaterBuddy leads exactly where features are rare, with every quick-logging surface free. It trails on drinks (14 of 20), caffeine (4), characters and challenges (8 each; the three most-rated apps are all gamified), languages, iCloud sync (6), weather goals (5, which its no-weather rule excludes anyway), themes or icons (5) and a Live Activity (2).

Two apps deserve watching. Water Log (Ellisapps) matches WaterBuddy's free surfaces almost one for one and sells drinks, custom cups, past-day logging and a Live Activity at $2.99/$19.99/$29.99, which makes it the closest product rival. Hydro Coach (992 ratings, 23rd) is the closest privacy twin: it declares "Data Not Collected", shows no ads, logs from a widget without unlocking, counts each drink's actual water and reminds only when behind ([Hydro Coach](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1459692731)). Waterllama has also shipped Monthly Recaps (2026-07), on-device AI daily and weekly recaps on iOS 27 (2026-09) and a daily highlight built on Apple's Foundation Models (2025-09), tiers unstated ([Waterllama page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1454778585)). That erodes the novelty of WaterBuddy's Premium insights and recap.

The business models cluster. Seventeen of the 20 sell subscriptions, fifteen run ads, eleven offer a lifetime or one-time unlock, seven sell ad removal on its own, and five sell weekly plans. Monthly prices cluster at $2.99–$4.99, yearly at $14.99–$39.99 (up to $69.99), and one-time unlocks most often at $19.99–$29.99. Most trials run three to seven days. **Logging itself is never paywalled.** What is gated is breadth (drinks, cups, per-drink factors), personality (characters, plants, fish, icons), convenience (editing past days, iCloud, sometimes the widget or the Watch) and freedom from ads. WaterBuddy's monthly price ties the cheapest serious plans, its yearly sits mid-pack and its lifetime is on the high side. Its paywall sits on analytics and coaching: longer history, patterns, AI insights, recap, export, schedule and pace. Rivals mostly give history away and rarely sell export (only the P App has it).

## Reviewers resent paywalled drinks and ads, and pay once for delight

The review team read 3,500 US reviews of the ten most-rated trackers (eight returned any), 2,346 of them dated 2025 or later, plus 350 UK reviews of the top three. The recent stream is 85.3% four- and five-star and mostly under 100 characters, so complaints are best read as shares of its 208 one- and two-star reviews. Every review that mentions money was read and coded by hand ([Waterllama feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1454778585/sortby=mostrecent/json), [My Water feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=964748094/sortby=mostrecent/json), and the research's hand-coded money tally, kept outside the repository).

Paywalls lead the complaints. **170 recent US reviews (7.2%), including 65 of the 208 negative ones (31.2%), object to paying or being pushed to pay.** In 84 of them the target is basic logging, and **51 of those 84 name drinks other than water**: milk, juice, coffee, tea, soda. Another 10 name custom amounts. In the UK, 13 of 18 such complaints are about drinks. Ads come second among negative reviews (27.9%), concentrated in the ad-funded apps (28.4% of TMEDILAB's reviews, 15% of Phoenix's), and they include fake virus warnings, a fake ransom demand and gambling ads ([TMEDILAB feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1478980974/sortby=mostrecent/json), [Phoenix feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=870372885/sortby=mostrecent/json)). Bugs follow at 13.9%. Reliability failures are rare but end relationships: 13 reviews report reminders that stop or never arrive, about six a broken Watch complication, 17 billing trouble (a trial charged as a year, no way to cancel, purchases not restored), and 11 resent features that used to be free.

Read together, this is one complaint with two faces: a water tracker should be free and quiet. Each business model draws its own share of it. The subscription apps (Waterllama, VGFIT, My Water, Plant Nanny) collect paywall anger, and the ad-funded ones collect ad anger. Paying to log still ranks first, as in the owner's October scan, but at a smaller share. The likely reason is the larger and more prompted sample, not a change in mood.

Requests are rare. Only about 5.5% of recent reviews contain a genuine one, and most ask to unlock what already exists. The top asks are to make paid features free (29 reviews, drinks first), custom amounts and own bottle sizes (16: 40 oz, a gallon, 16.9 oz, half a cup), and more drink types and contents such as electrolytes, sugar and caffeine (13). Then come paying once to remove ads (13) and reminder control (12: hours, quiet times, intervals, skipping a reminder right after a drink, and how much to drink to stay on pace), followed by one-time or cheaper pricing (11) and more characters (7). **No review asked for a Live Activity, iCloud backup, themes or dark mode, or a goal driven by weather or workouts.** Short five-star reviews rarely name features, and users who leave over a missing feature rarely write, so a missing request is weak evidence that a feature lacks value. It is still the only evidence there is.

Satisfied users praise outcomes and feel. The app makes them drink more (28.8% of positive reviews), it is cute or beautiful (15.5%), it is simple (12.1%) and its reminders help (11.0%); gamification is praised in 6.8%. Being free is praised more than twice as often (82 reviews) as paying (38), and the Watch is the one platform feature praised often.

The 38 happy payers bought concrete things on fair terms. Waterllama buyers cite its lifetime price at 75–83% off (about $10), unlimited drinks (one for a medical need), smart reminders, cuteness and supporting the developers. WaterMinder buyers cite icons and colours for drinks and its price; one calls under $8 a year a bargain. UK buyers cite exact cup capacities, cute graphics and £10 a year. Seventeen reviews praise a one-time or lifetime price, fourteen of them at Waterllama, and none praises a subscription as such. Recurring prices of about $30–$80 a year are the ones called too much: Plant Nanny's $60–70 in six reviews, My Water's $49.99–60 ([Plant Nanny feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1424178757/sortby=mostrecent/json)). **History, insights, export and recap images, the core of WaterBuddy's Premium, almost never appear.**

The money data agrees. On 2026-11-20 no dedicated hydration app was in the Health & Fitness top-grossing 100 of the US, UK, Germany, Japan or Brazil, nor of Canada, Australia, France, Italy, Spain or Ukraine. The money in "drink water" sits in calorie apps that also log water: MyFitnessPal is US #1 ([US grossing](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/topgrossingapplications/limit=200/genre=6013/json)). Third-party models estimate Waterllama at about **$100K–$160K a month** and WaterMinder at **$15K–$35K** ([Adapty, Waterllama](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/water-tracker-waterllama/), [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/water-tracker-waterllama/), [Adapty, WaterMinder](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/water-tracker/)). The median Health & Fitness app in Adapty's library of 615 paywalls makes about $5K a month. RevenueCat puts the median new subscription app at about **$72 a month a year after launch**, with 4.6% reaching $10K within two years ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)).

The two biggest earners by the available signals, Waterllama by revenue estimates and Plant Nanny by install base (no revenue estimate exists for it), both sell a character or growth game. Waterllama won Apple's 2022 Cultural Impact award for its cute characters and challenges, and Plant Nanny sells consumable clovers on top of its plants ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2022/11/app-store-awards-celebrate-the-best-apps-and-games-of-2022/)). WaterBuddy's analytics-and-coaching bundle looks more like WaterMinder's smaller, durable business. Because logging is never paid and the paywall appears only when the user opens it, WaterBuddy is freemium by design. It should expect conversion near the freemium median: about 2% of downloads paying by day 35, against 10–12% behind a hard paywall ([RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)). Motivation is the category's soft spot. In a 2025 MacRumors thread about 15 of 25 repliers used no app at all, and one quit once an app showed their intake was already fine ([MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/what-if-any-water-reminder-app-do-you-use.2456156/)). Reddit could not be read.

Put together, reviewers describe a gap no leading app fills: logging that is free, covers every drink, shows no ads and takes their own bottle. The free apps fund themselves with ads, and the ad-free ones lock drinks and amounts. WaterBuddy already covers three of the four: free logging everywhere, its own amounts through editable vessels and edits, and no ads. It lacks only drinks. And the one paid-style request that keeps the same shape, reminder control, describes reminders WaterBuddy already ships almost word for word: chosen hours and quiet days (set B's schedule), an hour's silence after a drink, and a pace line saying how much is still due.

## Build free drinks first, then sell caffeine, icons and themes

With zero ratings, no feature will move downloads as much as the listing and the rating prompt. So each feature is judged on three things: the demand evidence above; fit with WaterBuddy's constraints (no server, account, ads, location, weather or third-party SDKs, logging never paid, nothing lost when Premium lapses); and whether it amends a project rule. The lapse constraint gives a test worth stating: **nothing that changes the day's total or how a day is judged can sit behind Premium**, because a lapse would then rewrite figures the user has already seen. A per-drink hydration factor, if adopted, must therefore apply to everyone. Anything Premium adds should either be worked out from the logs or be a setting that a lapse freezes, as the reminder schedule already is ([rule 20-state](../../../.claude/rules/20-state.md)). The last column marks what needs the owner's approval before code.

| Candidate | Verdict | Tier | Demand evidence | Project rule it amends (needs the owner's approval) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Other drinks: tea, coffee, juice, milk | **Build first** | Free | 51 of 84 complaints about paying to log; 12 requests to free drinks; 14 of 20 rivals offer drinks, most behind a paywall; TMEDILAB is praised for free drinks | None, provided Apple Health still receives Water only and the CSV keeps its three columns; a drink column in the export or a non-water type in Health amends 70-privacy |
| Caffeine tracking | **Build second**, on top of drinks | Premium | 4 of 20 offer it; WaterMinder marked its caffeine chart Premium (2026-11); 20 caffeine mentions and 3 content requests in reviews | None, if worked out from drink logs and kept in the app; writing caffeine to Health is a second HealthKit type and amends 70-privacy |
| Alternate app icons | **Build early** (small) | Premium | Payers cite icons and colours (WaterMinder, My Water); personality is what the category sells; 5 of 20 offer themes or icons; no explicit requests | None found; a lapse keeps the chosen icon, as the reminder schedule is kept |
| Themes | Build after approval | Premium | "Cute, beautiful" is 15.5% of praise; no explicit requests | 60-design-system: every colour comes from `Aurora`, and its contrast figures are measured, not chosen |
| Goal raised by Health workouts | Later | Premium | 4 of 20 adjust for activity; adaptive goals are spreading among 2025–26 entrants; no requests | 70-privacy names workouts as needing an amendment and keeps Health a copy, never a source; 20-state judges every day against the current goal, so per-day goals would be needed |
| More than three vessels | Fold into drinks | Free | The 16 custom-amount requests are already met by editable vessels; locked custom amounts draw 10 complaints | 20-state and 25-shared-storage fix `Key.servings` at exactly three, with the widget following the middle one |
| More app languages | Once App Store Connect shows where installs come from | Free | 3 languages against a median of about 16; Waterllama grosses in Türkiye, Korea, Taiwan and China | 70-privacy lists exactly en, ru and uz (rule 15-project gives the procedure for adding one) |
| Live Activity | **Not now** | — | 2 of 20 offer one; no requests; one complaint about one | 70-privacy forbids a Live Activity showing wire-derived figures, and today's total counts Watch pours |
| iCloud backup and sync | **Not now** | — | 6 of 20 offer it; 3 sync requests, none for backup; 2 data-loss reports | 70-privacy bans CloudKit by name; it also contradicts the "No account · No cloud" promise |

Rule sources: [70-privacy](../../../.claude/rules/70-privacy.md), [60-design-system](../../../.claude/rules/60-design-system.md), [25-shared-storage](../../../.claude/rules/25-shared-storage.md), [15-project](../../../.claude/rules/15-project.md).

Drinks come first because their evidence is the strongest in the data and they sharpen the positioning. "Every drink free, no ads" turns the line competitors draw into WaterBuddy's headline, and it closes the gap users notice most when comparing apps. The cost is real: `WaterLog` gains a drink kind across a model the widget and the Watch also compile, and the owner has decisions to make. Does tea count fully toward the goal (for everyone)? Do the widget, Control Center and the Watch keep logging the Glass of water? They should: one serving, one tap. Does Health receive a drink's water? A built-in set (water, tea, coffee, juice, milk and "other") keeps every drink free to log. Personal drinks with their own names, colours and icons could join Premium as personalisation, the thing WaterMinder's payers cite, as long as no drink a person actually drinks becomes unloggable for free. Caffeine follows as a Premium extra built on those logs. Because it is worked out from what was poured, a lapse hides a view and loses nothing.

Alternate icons and themes are the cheapest step toward the half of the market that pays for delight. Icons are small work. Themes require re-measuring contrast under a design system that forbids retuning by eye. A character layer is the proven top earner, but it isn't among the candidates and would change what WaterBuddy is; icons and themes are its nearest relatives within the app's identity.

The workout-raised goal is on-device and respects the no-weather rule, but it is the costliest candidate: a HealthKit read the privacy rule names explicitly, a new meaning for the Health usage string, and per-day goals, all for a feature no review asked for. A library of more than three vessels is better folded into drinks and kept free, with each drink bringing its own default vessel. Any amount can already be logged for free, and custom amounts are the second most common thing reviewers resent having locked. New app languages should follow App Store Connect's country data, as the playbook advises, with the Watch's words kept in step. The two new listing localizations, en-GB and Russian, come first and need no app change. A Live Activity and iCloud sync have neither demand nor rule room, and iCloud would undercut the clearest promise the app makes.

Two tempting moves are better declined. An onboarding paywall, which Waterllama shows after goal set-up and WaterMinder at the start, would lift conversion toward hard-paywall rates. But it amends rule 70-privacy's paywall moment and invites the upsell complaints Waterllama already draws (13 of the 19 recorded). A tip jar, which both leaders sell, needs 70-privacy's StoreKit section to admit consumables, for little evidenced revenue.

The order follows. The next version carries the en-US, en-GB and Russian fields, the reordered screenshots and the comma-bug fix, plus the wider rating moment if the owner approves it. It goes through the playbook's release checklist, as the owner's global instructions require. Price tiers can go in at any time in App Store Connect, without a version. The release after that brings free drinks, with alternate icons as the first new Premium item, and then caffeine. Themes and the workout goal wait for their rules to be amended. Live Activity and iCloud stay parked until reviews or support mail ask for them.

## Conclusion

The research changes where WaterBuddy's next effort belongs. The product already sits in the gap reviewers describe, free and quiet with every logging surface, yet its store page tells neither Apple's ranking nor a visitor. The most consequential finding is mechanical: apps with zero ratings rank in the US top 10 when their names *start* with the searched phrase. That makes the name the one lever that can move rank before ratings arrive. Almost everything with the highest leverage (the fields, the screenshots, the price tiers, the comma bug, two new listing localizations) needs no rule change. The one rule change worth asking for first is the rating moment, because every other gain compounds through ratings.

The second shift concerns what to sell. Money in this category follows delight and breadth, not insight, so WaterBuddy's Premium sells what its rivals' users never mention. Making every drink free turns the category's most resented paywall into WaterBuddy's headline. Caffeine, icons and themes then give Premium a delight half without charging for logging. Progress will be slow and should be measured in rank and rating counts, not in a week of downloads. The UK, Canada, Australia and, through a Russian listing, Russia are where first ranks will show; the playbook's own example took about six months to reach a UK top 10 and nearly three years to hold a US top 5 (the playbook: the owner's `aso` skill, `SKILL.md`). A WaterMinder-sized business, estimated at $15K–$35K a month, is the realistic ceiling for an indie hydration app without a game layer, and WaterMinder has been on the store since 2013.
